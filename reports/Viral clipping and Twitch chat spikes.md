# Turn stream chat spikes into Shorts

As of November 2026 there are three ways to turn a video link into captioned vertical shorts. You can paste it into a commercial clipper (OpusClip, Vizard, Klap, Submagic, Reap). You can call one of those vendors' URL-to-clips APIs. Or you can self-host the pipeline that nearly every open-source clipper has settled on: **yt-dlp → faster-whisper word timestamps → LLM moment ranking → MediaPipe/PySceneDetect 9:16 reframe → ASS karaoke captions burned in by ffmpeg**. For Twitch chat-spike clipping, the important 2026 change is on Twitch's side. **Helix now has "Create Clip From VOD" (`POST /helix/videos/clips`) and "Get Clips Download" (`GET /helix/clips/downloads`)**. With these, a streamer-authorized app can cut a clip of up to 60 seconds ending at any VOD offset, which makes "start 30 s before the spike" possible through documented APIs. One gap remains: Helix has no API for past-VOD chat, so chat replay of other people's VODs still depends on Twitch's undocumented GraphQL endpoint. The Developer Agreement forbids using undocumented endpoints without written permission. Detection is easy to get right with robust statistics: unique chatters in a 10 s window, scored against a rolling median/MAD baseline, then peak-picked. The real difficulty is the 8–25 s lag between an on-screen moment and the chat peak, so a fixed pre-roll plus transcript snapping is required. For the empty Clipper repo, I recommend a Python pipeline with two modes. The compliant mode is streamer OAuth with EventSub chat and Helix clips. The "any link" mode uses yt-dlp and is explicitly unofficial. If you want results today without code, use OpusClip for arbitrary links and Eklipse (or Twitch's own voice/Auto Clips features) for your own channel.

## Commercial clippers now offer the same features, so source support and pricing decide

The general-purpose tools have converged on one feature set. A pasted link produces ranked clips with **animated word-level captions, emoji and keyword highlights, face-tracking 9:16 reframe, a 0–99 "virality" score, AI B-roll and auto-posting**. They differ in which sources they accept, how they bill, and whether they have an API. OpusClip accepts the broadest set of sources. Its help center lists YouTube, Twitch, Kick, Rumble, Medal.tv, X, Zoom, public MP4 URLs and more, with uploads up to 10 GB ([OpusClip Help](https://help.opus.pro/docs/article/what-import-sources-does-opusclip-support)), and it accepts inputs up to 10 hours and 30 GB ([OpusClip API Limitations](https://help.opus.pro/api-reference/limitation)). Clipping a stream while it is still live is a private-beta "Live Clipping" feature ([OpusClip Livestream Clip Maker](https://www.opus.pro/tools/livestream-clip-maker)). The virality score is useful for sorting, not for prediction. One reviewer discarded about 40% of clips and described the score as "a shortlist rather than a predictor" ([computertech](https://computertech.co/opus-clip-review/)).

The commercial landscape has shifted. Powder has shut down ([clipme](https://clipme.com/powder-alternative)). Munch now redirects to "Munch Studio", a social-marketing product with no standalone clipper ([kompozy](https://kompozy.io/reviews/munch)). vidyo.ai is now quso.ai, and Captions has rebranded as Mirage ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Captions_(app))). All pricing below comes from third-party reviews, often written by competitors, so check the vendor's own page before committing.

**Most general clippers bill per source minute, which makes Twitch VODs expensive.** A 4-hour VOD uses about 240 OpusClip credits, close to a whole Pro month (300). OpusClip Starter works out to roughly $6 per source hour, against about $0.25 per VOD hour for VOD-specialist token models such as Highlight Hunter ([frostytools](https://frostytools.com/blog/how-to-turn-twitch-vods-into-clips-without-editing/)).

The APIs have specific limits. OpusClip's `POST https://api.opus.pro/api/clip-projects` allows 30 requests per minute and 4 concurrent projects. It also has a hard **15-hour (900-credit) monthly quota**, after which it returns 403 until the 1st of the next month ([OpusClip API Limitations](https://help.opus.pro/api-reference/limitation)). Vizard's create endpoint is limited to **3 requests per minute and 20 per hour** ([Vizard Rate Limit](https://docs.vizard.ai/docs/rate-limit)). Klap charges $0.44 per input video, $0.32 per short and $0.48 per export, so ten exported shorts from one source cost about $8.44 ([Klap API](https://klap.app/api); [bloggingspark](https://www.bloggingspark.com/klap-api-vs-opusclip-api/)). None of these vendors documents whether `clips.twitch.tv` clip URLs behave differently from full VOD URLs. None of the big four uses chat at all.

Streamer-focused tools fill that gap only partly. Eklipse says a July 2026 engine update added chat spikes to its game-event and audio scoring ([Eklipse AI Highlights](https://eklipse.gg/features/ai-highlights/)). Its own older help page says the AI does not consider chat ([Eklipse help](https://eklipse.gg/help/does-the-ai-consider-my-chats-reactions-when-picking-highlights/)), and competitor ClipMe says the same ([ClipMe vs Eklipse](https://clipme.com/vs/eklipse)). Twitch is also moving into this space. It announced **"Auto Clips"** at TwitchCon Europe 2026, which generates captioned clips from "chat activity, vocal inflection, and on-screen events" (alpha, English channels, application required) ([streamer.guide](https://streamer.guide/blog/twitchcon-rotterdam-2026-new-features-1440p-clips-guilds)). "Clip That" voice clipping was rolling out to Partners and Affiliates in July 2026 ([Gaming Careers](https://gamingcareers.com/newsletters/twitch-clip-that-actually-works-now/)). Nobody has published an independent benchmark of chat-signal accuracy for any of these products.

## A self-hosted pipeline needs six permissive parts and one LLM

No permissively licensed open-source project combines Twitch/Kick partial downloads, chat-spike detection, speaker-aware facecam/gameplay layouts and animated captions. The closest is **Clips Kitty (ColinGPT9/clips-studio)**. It takes YouTube, Twitch VODs with chat replay, and Kick VODs. It uses faster-whisper large-v3-turbo, multimodal scoring with a local or OpenRouter LLM, YOLOv8 + TalkNet active-speaker crop, and a webcam+game layout. But it is **AGPL-3.0 and Windows-first** ([clips-studio](https://github.com/ColinGPT9/clips-studio)). Several other "open-source" clippers send their key step to a vendor API by default. The most-starred one, AI-Youtube-Shorts-Generator (6.1k stars, MIT), defaults to MuAPI and only optionally runs yt-dlp + faster-whisper locally ([repo](https://github.com/Anil-matcha/AI-Youtube-Shorts-Generator)). The 2023–24 pioneers, ClipsAI and Reelsfy, are abandoned ([ClipsAI](https://github.com/ClipsAI/clipsai)). So Clipper should be assembled from components, borrowing design ideas from these repos rather than depending on them.

**Downloading.** yt-dlp (about 208k stars, pushed 2026-11-15) remains the standard. `--download-sections "*01:23:10-01:24:40"` fetches only a time range from YouTube, Twitch and Kick VODs ([yt-dlp](https://github.com/yt-dlp/yt-dlp); [VideoHelp](https://forum.videohelp.com/showthread.php?p=2768027)). yt-dlp has Kick extractors for live channels, VODs and clips ([kick.py](https://github.com/yt-dlp/yt-dlp/blob/master/yt_dlp/extractor/kick.py)). YouTube is the fragile source. Full support now needs the `yt-dlp-ejs` component and a JavaScript runtime (Deno by default). The PO Token Guide recommends a PO-token provider plugin because several clients now serve "only SABR formats" ([PO Token Guide](https://github.com/yt-dlp/yt-dlp/wiki/PO-Token-Guide)). bgutil-ytdlp-pot-provider runs as a Docker sidecar on port 4416, but it warns that a token "does not guarantee bypassing 403 errors or bot checks" ([bgutil](https://github.com/Brainicism/bgutil-ytdlp-pot-provider)). The efficient pattern for long VODs has two stages. First fetch audio only, plus chat for Twitch, and score it. Then download video only for the chosen windows, with a few seconds of padding, and do the frame-accurate trim in the final render.

**Transcription.** faster-whisper with `word_timestamps=True` and `BatchedInferencePipeline` is the practical default. On an RTX 3070 Ti it transcribed 13 minutes of audio in **17 s** (fp16, batch 8), against 2m23s for openai/whisper ([faster-whisper](https://github.com/SYSTRAN/faster-whisper)). That puts a 3-hour VOD at about 4–5 minutes. WhisperX adds wav2vec2 alignment for tighter word boundaries and pyannote diarization for speaker-aware cropping ([WhisperX](https://github.com/m-bain/whisperX)). Parakeet TDT 0.6B v3 is much faster but supports fewer languages ([NVIDIA](https://build.nvidia.com/nvidia/parakeet-tdt-0_6b/modelcard)). Avoid building on OpenAI's hosted word timestamps. `timestamp_granularities` works only on `whisper-1`, which shuts down on **February 26, 2027** ([OpenAI STT guide](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/speech-to-text)). Hosted alternatives such as Deepgram and AssemblyAI return word timestamps for roughly $0.15–0.46 per hour ([AssemblyAI](https://assemblyai.com/blog/speech-to-text-api-pricing)).

**Moment selection.** Every serious clipper sends a timestamped transcript to an LLM and asks for N segments with scores, hooks and reasons. The 2026 improvement is adding non-text signals. Clips Kitty bins loudness spikes, laughter-like bursts, scene cuts, motion and face-area changes at 1 s and normalizes them per video. Where real audience data exists (Twitch chat replay measured by unique chatters, YouTube's "most replayed" heatmap), it adds "a small capped bonus". Finalists are reranked head-to-head because "relative judgment is much easier for a 7B model than absolute scoring". Its reported cost was **about $0.11 for a 2-hour video** on OpenRouter ([clips-studio](https://github.com/ColinGPT9/clips-studio)). JayWebtech/autoshorts warns that 3B/7B local models "lack the context reasoning and mathematical capabilities" to produce accurate timestamps ([autoshorts](https://github.com/JayWebtech/autoshorts)). Always snap the LLM's start and end times to real word boundaries from the transcript.

**Reframing.** The standard approach is to split shots with PySceneDetect, then decide per shot between TRACK (crop follows a face or person) and LETTERBOX/blur-fill. Smooth the crop within a shot and hard-cut between speakers. Autocrop-vertical documents this, along with an A/V drift bug caused by a 29.97-vs-30 fps mismatch that it fixed with `-vsync cfr` ([Autocrop-vertical](https://github.com/kamilstanuch/Autocrop-vertical)). For streams, use a facecam-over-gameplay `vstack` at about a 60/40 split ([HF clipbot](https://huggingface.co/spaces/imspsycho/clipbot-ffmpeg/blob/main/app.py)). Licensing affects the choice of detector. **Ultralytics YOLO is AGPL-3.0** ([ultralytics](https://github.com/ultralytics/ultralytics)), while MediaPipe is Apache-2.0. Use MediaPipe's Tasks `FaceDetector`, because legacy `mp.solutions` support ended in 2023 ([MediaPipe guide](https://developers.google.com/edge/mediapipe/solutions/guide)).

**Animated captions.** The fastest headless approach is to generate an ASS file from word timestamps and burn it in with libass during the same ffmpeg encode. Each page of 2–4 words is repeated once per word, with the active word recolored and scaled. Alternatively, use `\k` karaoke tags, which are in **centiseconds**, while `\t` animation times are in milliseconds. Mixing the two units is a common source of drift ([ffmpeg-kinetic-captions](https://claudskills.com/skills/ffmpeg-kinetic-captions/SKILL.md)). A single "Hormozi-style" line looks like `Dialogue: 0,0:00:01.20,0:00:01.48,Cap,,0,0,0,,THIS {\c&H00FFFF&\fscx115\fscy115}IS{\r} INSANE`. The script sets `PlayResX: 1080`, `PlayResY: 1920`, and ASS colors are written as BGR. For richer CSS animation and emoji there are pycaps (Playwright, MIT, "very alpha") ([pycaps](https://github.com/francozanardi/pycaps)) and Remotion's `createTikTokStyleCaptions` ([Remotion docs](https://github.com/remotion-dev/remotion/blob/main/packages/docs/docs/captions/create-tiktok-style-captions.mdx)). Remotion Lambda costs only about $0.02 per minute of video. However, Remotion requires a paid Company License for companies with more than three employees ([Remotion LICENSE](https://github.com/remotion-dev/remotion/blob/main/LICENSE.md); [Lambda cost](https://github.com/remotion-dev/remotion/blob/main/packages/docs/docs/lambda/cost-example.mdx)).

**Render settings.** Output 1080×1920 H.264 High, yuv420p, constant 30 fps (keep 60 fps for gameplay), AAC 48 kHz, `+faststart`, at about 10 Mbps for 1080p30 per YouTube's guidance ([YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1722171?hl=en)). Normalize loudness with two-pass `loudnorm` to about −14 LUFS and −1 dBTP. TikTok and Instagram do not publish loudness targets ([OpenClip LUFS](https://openclip.app/learn/audio-normalization)).

## Chat spikes trail the moment by 8–25 seconds, so a pre-roll is mandatory

**Getting chat data depends on whether the stream is live or a past VOD.** For live chat there are two official paths. Legacy IRC (`wss://irc-ws.chat.twitch.tv`) is still supported and carries `tmi-sent-ts` millisecond timestamps. EventSub `channel.chat.message` is the path Twitch recommends. It requires `user:read:chat` + `user:bot`, and `channel:bot` from the broadcaster when using app tokens ([Twitch IRC migration](https://dev.twitch.tv/docs/chat/irc-migration/)). Twitch has "no plan to remove IRC", but new features go to EventSub ([Twitch forum](https://discuss.dev.twitch.com/t/available-today-twitch-chat-on-eventsub-an-api-for-sending-chat-and-the-conduit-transport-method-for-eventsub/54596)). A **100-channel concurrent join limit** applies to both paths unless the account is the broadcaster or a moderator, or the broadcaster has granted `channel:bot` ([Twitch forum](https://discuss.dev.twitch.com/t/giving-broadcasters-control-concurrent-join-limits-for-irc-and-eventsub/54997)). Anonymous `justinfan` IRC reads still work and are used by chat-downloader, but Twitch does not document them ([chat-downloader](https://github.com/xenova/chat-downloader/blob/master/chat_downloader/sites/twitch.py)).

For past VODs, **no official API exists**. Twitch staff say "there is no API endpoint for getting historic chat data" ([Twitch forum](https://discuss.dev.twitch.tv/t/how-to-access-users-full-chat-logs/30221)), and the Kraken rechat endpoint has been dead since 2022 ([Twitch forum](https://discuss.dev.twitch.tv/t/legacy-twitch-api-v5-i-e-kraken-shutdown-reminder-february-28-2022/36589)). Every working tool calls the undocumented `gql.twitch.tv` operation `VideoCommentsByOffsetOrCursor` with a persisted-query hash and a non-web Client-ID. TwitchDownloaderCLI `chatdownload --id <vod> -o chat.json` produces JSON whose `content_offset_seconds` values are already VOD-relative ([ChatDownloader.cs](https://github.com/lay295/TwitchDownloader/blob/master/TwitchDownloaderCore/ChatDownloader.cs); [ChatRoot.cs](https://github.com/lay295/TwitchDownloader/blob/master/TwitchDownloaderCore/TwitchObjects/ChatRoot.cs)). Cursor paging can fail with "failed integrity check", and offset-based paging is the usual workaround ([ZetHor3](https://github.com/ZetHor3/TwitchChatDownloader)). justlog/rustlog archives such as logs.ivr.fi only cover channels they were already logging ([rustlog](https://github.com/boring-nick/rustlog/blob/master/README.md)). So the durable design is to **record chat live** for channels you care about and use GQL only as a fallback. The notes did not verify how to read Kick chat (a Pusher websocket versus Kick's official API), so that needs a spike of its own.

**Detection.** Mature reference implementations converge on one algorithm, and it is simple. serdarakbay/hype-clipper documents it most clearly. It counts **unique chatters in a rolling 10 s window**, compares them against a rolling median/MAD baseline after a 3-minute warmup, and triggers at a score of 3.0 or a 2.0–3.0× multiplier over baseline. It waits for the peak to resolve, firing on a 70% drop from the peak or after 8 s with no new maximum (60 s cap), then adds 20 s of pre-roll ([hype-clipper](https://github.com/serdarakbay/hype-clipper)). VoXCluTcH adds spam resistance. Its score is `0.45·velocity + 0.35·author-entropy + 0.20·reaction-ratio`, so many distinct chatters outweigh one spammer, and it fuses this 70/30 with per-second audio RMS measured against a 40 s median ([VoXCluTcH](https://github.com/Rohi56u/VoXCluTcH)). twitch-chat-highlights contributes emote categories and non-maximum suppression through a minimum bucket spacing ([twitch-chat-highlights](https://github.com/joaohenggeler/twitch-chat-highlights)). The research consensus is that chat alone is useful but noisy and lagged. Chat plus video is more robust ([Fu et al., EMNLP 2017](https://aclanthology.org/D17-1102/)). Learned emote embeddings with video features detected popular clips with **0.8261 accuracy** ([dscig](https://github.com/dscig/twitch-highlight-detection)). Lightor (ICDE 2020) treats the chat-to-moment lag as a first-class problem and shifts predictions earlier ([Lightor](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/uploads/prod/2020/02/icde20lightor.pdf)).

| Stage | Recommended starting value | Reference |
|---|---|---|
| Time base | 1 s bins; drop bots (Nightbot, StreamElements…), `!commands`, sub/raid/cheer notices; cap 1 message per user per 2 s | [hype-clipper](https://github.com/serdarakbay/hype-clipper) failure cases |
| Core signal | unique chatters per 10 s plus weighted reaction tokens (KEKW/LUL, Pog/LETS GO, monkaS/WTF, "clip it" ×3) | [hype-clipper](https://github.com/serdarakbay/hype-clipper); [VoXCluTcH](https://github.com/Rohi56u/VoXCluTcH) |
| Baseline | rolling 300 s median, scale 1.4826·MAD floored at max(1, 0.1·median); minimum of 5 unique chatters per 10 s | [hype-clipper](https://github.com/serdarakbay/hype-clipper) |
| Spam | each distinct normalized message counts √n times per 10 s; author-entropy weight; suppress for 90 s after a raid | [VoXCluTcH](https://github.com/Rohi56u/VoXCluTcH) |
| Peaks (VOD) | `find_peaks(height=3.0, prominence=2.0, distance=30)`; merge within 20–30 s; keep the top 3–8 per hour | [SciPy](https://docs.scipy.org/doc/scipy/reference/generated/scipy.signal.find_peaks.html) |
| Peaks (live) | causal windows; fire on a 70% drop or 8 s with no new max; 45 s cooldown | [hype-clipper](https://github.com/serdarakbay/hype-clipper) |

The window values above come from the cited repos. The weights and the exact combination are engineering starting points, not validated constants. No project publishes precision/recall against human clips.

**Boundaries.** The chat peak lags the on-screen moment because of viewer video latency (a few seconds in Low Latency mode, about 10–15 s in Normal mode) ([BoostStream](https://booststream.net/blog/twitch-stream-delay)), plus reaction and typing time, plus the time it takes chat to build. Together that puts the cause **about 8–25 s before the peak**. hype-clipper estimates 5–15 s of reaction lag and uses a 20 s pre-roll ([hype-clipper](https://github.com/serdarakbay/hype-clipper)). A 30 s pre-roll is therefore a sensible, conservative default. Implement it as `start = min(onset − 20 s, peak − 30 s)`, where `onset` is the first second above about 1.5 robust-z. Then snap the start back to the nearest Whisper segment start, or to an audio silence within ±3 s, so the clip never opens mid-word. End at `max(peak + 10 s, score < 1.0) + 3 s` and clamp the total to 20–60 s. Thirty seconds of setup is long for a short-form hook, so either trim the setup at the editing stage or open with a cold-open of the reaction. No retention data was found to settle which works better.

The calibration data you need is already available through the API. **Helix Get Clips returns each clip's `vod_offset` and `view_count`** ([twitch-api-swagger](https://github.com/DmitryScaletta/twitch-api-swagger)). A channel's most-viewed human-made clips therefore serve as free ground truth for tuning thresholds and learning that channel's typical chat lag. dscig used the same idea for academic labels ([dscig](https://github.com/dscig/twitch-highlight-detection)).

## Twitch's 2026 clip endpoints make the compliant path practical

Twitch now has four clip endpoints. Two of them make streamer-authorized spike clipping cleaner than downloading VODs.

**`POST /helix/clips`** (live; scope `clips:edit`, available to any viewer's token) now accepts `title` and `duration` (5–60 s, default 30). It "captures up to 90 seconds" around the call: a call at 4:00 covers roughly 2:35 to 4:05 ([twitch-api-swagger](https://github.com/DmitryScaletta/twitch-api-swagger); [twitch-rs](https://github.com/twitch-rs/twitch_api/blob/main/src/helix/endpoints/clips/create_clip.rs)). A 60 s clip ends about 5 s after the call, so to start 30 s before the peak the detector must call **within about 25 s of the peak**. hype-clipper's 8-second peak wait fits. The older `has_delay` flag still appears in Twitch's guide but no longer appears in the generated reference ([Twitch Clips guide](https://dev.twitch.tv/docs/api/clips/)).

**`POST /helix/videos/clips`** ("Create Clip From VOD", scopes `editor:manage:clips` or `channel:manage:clips`) takes `vod_id`, a `vod_offset` where the clip **ends**, and a `duration` of up to 60 s. It also works on "earlier in the current stream" ([twitch-rs](https://github.com/twitch-rs/twitch_api/blob/main/src/helix/endpoints/clips/create_clip_from_vod.rs)). For a spike peaking at P, set `vod_offset = P + 30` and `duration = 60` to get exactly `[P − 30, P + 30]`, with no timing race.

**`GET /helix/clips/downloads`** returns temporary `landscape_download_url` and `portrait_download_url` files. It takes up to 10 clip IDs per call, is limited to 100 requests per minute, and requires broadcaster or editor scopes ([twitch-rs](https://github.com/twitch-rs/twitch_api/blob/main/src/helix/endpoints/clips/get_clips_download.rs)). Twitch staff explain the restriction directly: streamers "should have control over who can download their content" ([Twitch forum](https://discuss.dev.twitch.com/t/get-clips-download-twitch-api-endpoint-now-available-in-open-beta/64334)). One developer reports that downloads come back as 1080p60 H.264 even from 1440p HEVC streams ([Twitch forum](https://discuss.dev.twitch.com/t/get-clips-download-returns-1080p-h-264-for-clips-from-a-1440p-hevc-stream-is-source-quality-available/65261)).

Stream markers (`POST /helix/streams/markers`, `channel:manage:broadcast`) are an official way to bookmark spikes for later review.

Clips longer than 60 s, or clips from channels that have not authorized you, require video downloaded outside the official API. **There is no official VOD download API.** yt-dlp, streamlink and TwitchDownloader all use undocumented GQL `PlaybackAccessToken` and Usher HLS playlists, and streamlink sometimes needs a browser-generated client-integrity token ([yt-dlp twitch.py](https://github.com/yt-dlp/yt-dlp/blob/master/yt_dlp/extractor/twitch.py); [streamlink twitch.py](https://github.com/streamlink/streamlink/blob/master/src/streamlink/plugins/twitch.py)). VODs also expire. Past broadcasts are kept for 7 days for regular accounts, 14 for Affiliates and 60 for Partners/Turbo/Prime ([Archive Team](https://wiki.archiveteam.org/index.php/Twitch)). Get Videos exposes `muted_segments` from Twitch Audio Recognition ([twitch-api-swagger](https://github.com/DmitryScaletta/twitch-api-swagger)). Muted sections are silent in downloads and are a strong sign of copyrighted music, so skip them.

The legal picture falls into three tiers. The Developer Services Agreement forbids accessing "undocumented Program Materials" without written permission. It also limits storage of Twitch Content to **24 hours** unless authorized ([Twitch DSA](https://www.twitch.tv/p/en/legal/developer-agreement/)). The consumer ToS bans scraping and circumventing access controls ([Twitch ToS](https://www.twitch.tv/p/en/legal/terms-of-service/)). Twitch staff have said on the forum that an OAuth consent flow makes storing a user's data beyond 24 hours compliant. That is a forum statement, not a contract term ([Twitch forum](https://discuss.dev.twitch.com/t/storage-of-data-from-twitch-api/63884)). Reposting is a separate question from API compliance. Twitch's share-to-TikTok/YouTube license covers only its own share feature, which streamers can disable ([Dexerto](https://dexerto.com/twitch/new-twitch-clip-feature-could-streamers-in-trouble-with-tiktok-youtube-2904523)). Uploading someone else's stream needs their permission. Statutory damages run $750–$30,000 per work, and three YouTube strikes terminate a channel ([Lumina Clippers](https://luminaclippers.com/blog/is-clipping-legal), a vendor and not legal advice). Music licensed through Twitch's DJ program does not carry over to clips shared off Twitch ([NetInfluencer](https://www.netinfluencer.com/twitch-adds-clips-to-its-dj-program-but-licensed-music-wont-come-along/)). The notes did not examine YouTube's or Kick's terms on downloading. Review them before you offer "any link" ingestion as a hosted service.

|---|---|---|---|
| A. Streamer-authorized | EventSub `channel.chat.message` / IRC recorded live | Create Clip From VOD + Get Clips Download (≤60 s) | Documented APIs; OAuth consent covers storage |
| B. Viewer, live only | EventSub or IRC | `POST /helix/clips` (clip URL only, no official file) | Official API; reposting still needs the streamer's permission |
| C. Any VOD or link | GQL chat replay (TwitchDownloader/chat-downloader) | yt-dlp / streamlink / TwitchDownloader sections | Unofficial; contrary to the DSA's undocumented-API clause and the ToS scraping ban; can break whenever Twitch changes hashes or adds integrity checks |

## Recommended Clipper architecture and build plan

Build Clipper as a **Python monorepo**. The essential libraries (faster-whisper, WhisperX, SciPy, MediaPipe, PySceneDetect) are Python, and ffmpeg does the heavy media work. Put a job queue behind a small API and design for the two-stage "score cheaply, download narrowly" flow. Twitch compliance is a per-source adapter choice, not a separate product, so the pipeline should be source-agnostic. Twitch Mode A is the default for any public or monetized use. Mode C stays behind an explicit "personal use / unofficial" flag. The component choices below are my synthesis of the evidence above. The licenses are as cited.

| Component | Choice | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Ingest adapters | `twitch_helix` (Mode A/B), `ytdlp` (YouTube, Kick, Twitch Mode C), `local` | One interface: `get_audio()`, `get_chat()`, `get_window(start, end)` |
| Chat recorder | Long-running EventSub WebSocket (twurple or twitchio v3) writing per-second rows to Postgres; IRC `tmi-sent-ts` fallback | Avoids GQL; VOD offset = `sent_ts − stream.started_at` |
| VOD chat fallback | TwitchDownloaderCLI `chatdownload` (pinned version) | Only working path for past VODs; flagged unofficial |
| Signals | Chat robust-z (table above), per-second RMS dB vs 40 s median, PySceneDetect cuts | Cheap prefilter before any LLM call |
| Transcription | faster-whisper `large-v3-turbo`, batched, `word_timestamps=True`, VAD on; transcribe only ±90 s around candidates for Twitch | About 45× realtime on an 8 GB GPU |
| Ranking | LLM gets candidate transcripts plus the signal timeline; strict JSON `{start,end,hook,title,score,reason}`; pairwise rerank; snap to word boundaries | Pattern from Clips Kitty and autoshorts |
| Boundaries | `start = min(onset−20, peak−30)`, snap to segment start or silence; clamp 20–60 s; skip `muted_segments` | Lag model and Helix constraints |
| Reframe | MediaPipe Tasks face detection plus a smoothed crop; per-streamer facecam rectangle for a `vstack` 60/40 layout; blur-fill fallback | Apache-2.0, avoids the AGPL of Ultralytics |
| Captions | ASS generator (word-pop and karaoke templates, Montserrat ExtraBold, Noto Color Emoji) burned in by libass | Same encode pass, effectively free |
| Render | One ffmpeg pass: trim, reframe, `ass=`, `loudnorm` −14/−1, H.264 High 1080×1920, NVENC with libx264 fallback | Platform-safe defaults |
| Review and publish | Web review queue (approve, retitle, nudge start/end), then YouTube/TikTok/IG upload | LLM scores are a shortlist, not a verdict |

The build order follows the evidence about what is hard. **Week 1 is an offline CLI** for a single Twitch VOD. TwitchDownloaderCLI provides chat, the detector produces ranked spikes, yt-dlp `--download-sections` fetches padded windows, and ffmpeg renders blur-fill 9:16 with ASS word-by-word captions from faster-whisper. That gives a working "spike → captioned Short" loop in days. **Week 2 adds calibration.** Pull the channel's top clips via Get Clips (`vod_offset`, `view_count`) and measure how many the detector recovers within ±30 s, then tune the z threshold, prominence and per-channel lag, optionally with chat-audio cross-correlation. **Week 3 adds quality.** Add the transcript-plus-signals LLM rerank, facecam/gameplay split layouts and dead-air trimming. auto-editor's audio threshold works for trimming ([auto-editor](https://github.com/WyattBlue/auto-editor)). **Week 4 adds the compliant live mode.** Streamer OAuth (`channel:manage:clips`, `user:read:chat`/`channel:bot`, `channel:manage:broadcast`), the EventSub recorder, live causal detection, and Create Clip From VOD with `vod_offset = peak + 30` and Get Clips Download. Video ingest then needs no unofficial endpoints. **Later**, add the generic-link path for YouTube and Kick, where there is no chat. It runs the same pipeline driven by transcript, audio and scene signals, with the bgutil PO-token sidecar for YouTube. After that, add the review UI and publishing.

Watch for these pitfalls. Keep yt-dlp updated weekly, because YouTube and Twitch internals change. Process VODs within the 7–14-day retention window. Force constant frame rate to avoid A/V drift. Treat raids, hype trains and gift-sub bursts as non-content spikes.

**If you just want results today**, the fastest no-code route depends on whose content it is. For **any link** (YouTube, a Twitch VOD, Kick), paste it into **OpusClip**. It has the broadest source support, including Kick, and gives captioned, reframed, scored 9:16 clips with auto-posting from the $15 Starter plan ([OpusClip Help](https://help.opus.pro/docs/article/what-import-sources-does-opusclip-support); [lumiclip](https://www.lumiclip.ai/blog/opus-clip-pricing)). Vizard at $29 is the close alternative. For **your own Twitch or Kick channel**, connect **Eklipse** ($24.99/month), which processes each stream automatically when it ends ([Eklipse](https://eklipse.gg/features/twitch-clip-maker/); [Eklipse Kick](https://blog.eklipse.gg/streaming-tips/how-to-clip-on-kick-2.html)). Its chat-spike detection is disputed, so verify it on a test stream. Also turn on Twitch's own "Clip That" voice clipping and apply for the Auto Clips alpha. If a chat-spike signal specifically matters to you, ClipMe and Klypra market it, but those claims are self-published and unverified ([clipme](https://clipme.com/twitch-clipper); [klypra](https://klypra.app/blog/best-ai-tool-to-clip-twitch-streams)).

## Conclusion

The technical problem is mostly solved. Word-timestamped transcription, LLM ranking, reframing and karaoke captions are commodity parts, and a robust-z chat detector fits in about a hundred lines. What decides whether Clipper is viable is **data access and rights**. Twitch's 2025–26 clip endpoints changed the economics. A streamer-authorized tool can now detect a spike from live EventSub chat and have Twitch cut and serve the exact `[peak − 30 s, peak + 30 s]` window, with no scraping at all. The price is the 60-second ceiling and the need for streamer consent. Tools built on clipping strangers' VODs carry a structural risk: undocumented GQL chat, Usher downloads and reposting without permission can each break or be enforced against at any time.

That points to a positioning choice. Chat as a signal is the one thing the major commercial clippers do not use, and Twitch's own Auto Clips alpha will soon cover the generic version for its streamers. Clipper's defensible advantage is therefore the combination of a per-channel lag and threshold calibration learned from each channel's own top clips, multimodal fusion, and a consent-based workflow that streamers or their clip editors can safely run at scale. Validate the calibration loop first. The detector's precision against real human clips has not been measured publicly and is still unknown.
